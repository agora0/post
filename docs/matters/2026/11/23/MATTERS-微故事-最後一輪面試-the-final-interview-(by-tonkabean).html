微故事: 最後一輪面試


面試室的空調很冷，氣氛比空調更冷。

 這是（巨頭企業壓力舒緩部門）的最終面試，老闆親自主持，因為他聽說（大笑瑜伽）能為公司帶來莫大的商機——提升效率、降低醫療成本、誰不喜歡笑容滿面的服務？總之就是錢。

 對面坐著一個神情憔悴的中年男人，西裝皺得像梅菜。


老闆翻著履歷，眉頭越皺越深："你說你有很豐富的（銷售快樂）的經驗？過去十年，你賣掉房子，一棟又一棟，賣掉名車，一輛又一輛，最後連老婆的珠寶手袋都賣光了。"


應徵者笑得有點羞澀："是的，我已經跌過無數次，每次走到絕路，潛意識都激發起我的"銷售"念頭。我發現，每賣走一件東西，我就笑一下；賣得越多，笑得越厲害。到最後什麼都不剩了，我反而笑到停不下來。我太太本來幾乎瘋了，哭得死去活來，但她看著看著我，竟然也笑了。我們在空蕩蕩的家裡，笑聲像回音谷一樣，來回撞擊。

老闆眼神從輕蔑轉為專注。他想起了大笑瑜伽的原理：笑聲本身就是一種呼吸運動，幫助我們延長呼氣，排走肺底積存的舊空氣。眼前這個人，竟在人生最低處誤打誤撞學會了最深層的釋放。


"你通過了。" 老闆遞出合約，"你是首席大笑瑜伽導師。"

應徵者接過，平靜地說："謝謝。"

老闆挑眉："你破產又得此重任，就只有一句謝謝？"

應徵者眨眨眼，看看闊大的辦公室內一張家庭照，再看向老闆："謝謝您，爸爸。"


他想起大笑瑜伽所說，無條件的笑聲能夠連結人心，讓人建立如家人般的緊密關係。但他此刻只覺得，這個連結未免太荒謬。

老闆的筆掉在地上。他別過臉，幾乎聽不見地嘀咕了一句："這傢伙走出去，最好別讓人知道是我兒子..."

應徵者苦笑。這句話，他聽過——幾乎一字不差。在笑瑜伽課堂上，導師，都是用同一種語調。


完

Micro-story: The Final Interview  (by Tonkabean)


The air conditioning in the interview room was cold, but the atmosphere was even colder.


This was the final interview for the "Stress Relief Department" at a corporate giant. The boss was hosting it himself because he heard that Laughter Yoga could bring massive business opportunities—boosting efficiency, lowering healthcare costs, and who doesn't like service with a big smile? In short: big money.


Sitting across from him was a tired, haggard middle-aged man whose suit was as wrinkled as pickled mustard greens.


Flipping through the resume, the boss frowned deeper and deeper. "You say you have rich experience in 'selling happiness'? Over the past decade, you sold your houses, one after another; your luxury cars, one after another; and finally, even your wife’s jewelry and designer bags."


The applicant gave a timid smile. "Yes, I’ve fallen countless times. Every time I hit rock bottom, my subconscious triggered my urge to 'sell.' I realized that every time I sold something, I laughed a little. The more I sold, the harder I laughed. In the end, when nothing was left, I couldn't stop laughing. My wife almost lost her mind, crying her eyes out. But as she watched me laugh, she actually started laughing, too. In our empty house, our laughter bounced back and forth like an echo in a valley."


The boss’s eyes turned from contempt to deep focus. He remembered the principle of Laughter Yoga: laughter itself is a breathing exercise that helps prolong exhalation and flush out stale air trapped deep in the lungs. The man in front of him had stumbled into the deepest form of emotional release at the lowest point of his life.


"You passed," the boss said, handing over the contract. "You are our Chief Laughter Yoga Instructor."


The applicant took it and said calmly, "Thank you."


The boss raised an eyebrow. "You're broke, yet you just landed a dream job, and all you have to say is 'thank you'?"


The applicant blinked, looked at a family photo on the wall in the spacious office, then looked back at the boss. "Thank you, Dad."

He recalled what Laughter Yoga taught—unconditional laughter connects people, building tight, family-like bonds. But right now, he just found this connection utterly absurd.


The boss dropped his pen onto the floor. Turning his face away, he mumbled almost inaudibly, "When this guy steps out of here, he better not tell anyone he’s my son..."


The applicant smiled bitterly. He had heard these exact words before—almost word for word. In the Laughter Yoga class, his teacher used that very same tone.


End
